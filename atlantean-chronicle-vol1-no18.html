Washington Tells Gulf Allies to Brace for Strikes After Election Day

US officials have warned Middle East partners to prepare for a possible "brief but intense" campaign against Iran once the November 3 midterms are past, according to the Wall Street Journal and the AP. Iran says tankers have hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz, Qatar has put a new draft deal on the table, and Tehran's formal answer is expected as soon as Saturday. President Trump says no decision has been made.
By The Chronicle International Desk · October 25–30, 2026

The warning came in private. The Wall Street Journal reported on October 28 that Washington had told Gulf states to expect a "brief but intense" campaign against Iran after the midterms, and that a Patriot air-defense system was being shipped from Texas to Israel. The AP, citing two diplomats, reported on October 29 that the action could last several days and that allies were urged to take precautions, because Iran's response is expected to include strikes on energy facilities and other critical infrastructure. President Trump has said he will not attack before November 3 and has not made a final decision. According to the Journal, CENTCOM commander Adm. Brad Cooper briefed him in person on military options on Thursday.

At sea, the claims came from Tehran. On Tuesday, October 27, Iran's army said an oil tanker hit naval mines and caught fire after taking a route Iran had not authorized. On Thursday the Revolutionary Guard said three more tankers had struck mines on "the unauthorized southern route," and Iran's navy later reported another tanker damaged east of the strait. None of the mine strikes has been independently confirmed, and the counts vary by outlet. The UK Maritime Trade Operations center separately reported a tanker struck by a projectile on Thursday, with no injuries reported. CENTCOM says it has cleared the strait of mines; an IRGC spokesman answered on X, "Prove your claim of mine-clearing in the Strait of Hormuz with independent evidence, not a statement!"

The diplomatic track moved in the same week. Qatari mediators handed Iran a new draft deal on Sunday, October 25, according to Reuters. Officials from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey met Iranian counterparts in Tehran on Monday, and Qatar's emir met Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian in Moscow on Tuesday. "The ball is now in Iran's court," said Qatari foreign ministry official Ibrahim Al-Hashmi. A senior Iranian official told Reuters on Wednesday that Qatar had brought "unacceptable" ideas and that Tehran would show no flexibility on the nuclear issue. The same day Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi wrote that Iran "continues to give diplomacy a chance to succeed." Tasnim said Iran's core conditions stand: an end to the US blockade of its ports, sanctions relief and the release of frozen assets. An official told CBS News a formal response is expected by the end of the week, most likely Saturday.

A second channel opened as well. Vice President JD Vance sent a letter to Iranian security chief Mohsen Rezaei seeking to restart talks, the Journal and the New York Times reported. Trump played it down, telling Bloomberg it concerned humanitarian aid because "they need help with medical supplies."

The war, now eight months old, has a price tag. CENTCOM put the cost at about $47 billion, including $29 billion to replace munitions, and the Pentagon lists 23 US service members killed, 11 by hostile fire. On Wednesday Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth fired Lt. Gen. Brett Sylvia, the Joint Staff's head of strategy and plans, over alleged leaks of classified information. In Yemen, the Houthis struck Aden's airport on Wednesday, and Saudi forces said they shot down two ballistic missiles fired toward Riyadh on Thursday.

Israel Votes, and No One Wins: Netanyahu's Bloc Eight Seats Short

The Chronicle International Desk · October 27–30, 2026

Israel. With the count from Tuesday's election complete, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud is the largest party with 27 seats, ahead of Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar with 21 and Naftali Bennett's B'Yachad with 12, according to the Jerusalem Post, citing Central Elections Committee figures. Netanyahu's bloc of Likud, Shas, United Torah Judaism, Otzma Yehudit and Religious Zionism holds 53 seats, eight short of the 61 needed to govern. Eisenkot's opposition bloc holds 48. Arab parties won an unprecedented 19 seats. CNN said the result points to weeks of coalition talks or another election. Netanyahu has alleged fraud in the Arab-Israeli vote, a claim authorities dispute. He remains caretaker prime minister, and the new Knesset convenes early next month.

Serbia. A week after Aleksandar Vucic conceded, a partial count from 8,077 of 8,392 polling stations gave the Student List 52.54% to 39.29% for his United Serbia list, enough for a majority on its own in the 250-seat parliament, Al Jazeera reported. The head of state broadcaster RTS has resigned, and organizers are calling for audits of the public sector. No prime-ministerial candidate has been named. A government must be formed within 90 days of parliament convening. Final certified results have not been published.

Brazil. President Lula won a fourth term in the October 25 runoff with about 51% to roughly 48% for Flávio Bolsonaro, who has not conceded and has traveled to the United States. "I did not flee Brazil," he said.

Ukraine: A Six-Day Truce Gets Buses Into Oleshky

The Chronicle International Desk · October 27–30, 2026

Oleshky. No. 17 reported the localized ceasefire around Russian-occupied Oleshky in Kherson. It began Tuesday, has held four days, and runs through Sunday evening, November 1, the AP reported. On Friday a convoy of 25 buses, ambulances and aid trucks entered the town, where about 2,000 residents remain; two people were killed while routes were being demined. Moscow and Kyiv issued identical statements: "Everyone who wishes to leave Oleshky will have the opportunity to do so safely." The White House credits First Lady Melania Trump with brokering the mission.

Elsewhere. The fighting did not pause. Reuters reported that a Russian drone strike on a train station in Dnipro killed at least 14 on Thursday, and Russia said it downed more than 1,000 Ukrainian drones overnight into Friday. The Wall Street Journal reports that Kyiv paused strikes on Russian refineries after weeks of US pressure. On Wednesday President Zelensky set out his agenda for the next US-brokered round: a pause in strikes on energy and commercial shipping, more prisoner exchanges, and the format of a possible summit. No date for the November talks has been confirmed.

Forty-Six Million Early Votes, and the Map Keeps Moving

The Chronicle National Desk · October 26–30, 2026

More than 46 million Americans had voted early by Friday morning, according to the University of Florida Election Lab, passing the 45.9 million early votes cast in the entire 2022 midterm. A week ago one tracker counted 24.3 million; the Washington Post put the total at 39 million on Wednesday. Election Lab director Michael McDonald said early data point to turnout on the order of 2018's 53%, the highest midterm rate since 1914. In states that report party registration, Democrats account for 43.9% of early voters and Republicans 31.3%; several large states, including Texas, do not report it.

The ratings moved again. On Friday the Cook Political Report shifted 12 more House races toward Democrats and now lists 18 House toss-ups. It rates seven Senate races as toss-ups: Alaska, Iowa, Kansas, Maine, Michigan, Ohio and Texas. Democrats need a net gain of four seats to win the Senate. A Reuters/Ipsos poll taken October 22–26 found Democrats ahead 50% to 41% on the generic ballot, and 73% of adults said the cost of living is headed in the wrong direction. The president's approval stays historically low, from about 30% in Pew's early-October survey to 37.4% in the Silver Bulletin average.

The candidates spent the week where the margins are. Trump held two Texas rallies on Tuesday for Ken Paxton, whose race against Democrat James Talarico is described as virtually tied, and rallied in Ohio on Wednesday, where more than $400 million has been spent or reserved on the Brown–Husted Senate race. Kansas has drawn about $71 million in ads, the most of any Senate race in state history. Former President Obama campaigned in Atlanta with Sen. Jon Ossoff on Wednesday (see Local).

The Fed Holds, Two Governors Dissent, and December Looms Larger

The Chronicle National Desk · October 28–30, 2026

The Federal Open Market Committee held its target range at 3.75%–4.00% on Wednesday, as markets expected. The vote was 10–2: Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favor of a quarter-point hike. September's vote had been unanimous. The statement upgraded growth to "expanding at a strong pace" and dropped September's language that a hike would support a timelier return to 2%, replacing it with "More information will help set the stance for a timely return to 2%." At his press conference Chair Kevin Warsh said, "It's our responsibility to get inflation down," and that while financial conditions are tighter, "I'm unwilling and unable to call them tight."

Thursday's data kept the pressure on. September's PCE price index rose 0.5% on the month and 3.6% on the year, up from 3.4% in August; core PCE rose 3.1%. Spending grew 0.5% against 0.2% income growth, and the saving rate fell to 3.8%, the lowest in nearly four years. The advance estimate of third-quarter GDP showed the economy growing at a 3.4% annual rate, up from 2.2% in the second quarter. Weekly jobless claims were 194,000. CME futures put the odds of a December 9 hike near 85%, up from 78% a week ago, and on Friday two regional Fed presidents said rates may need to rise further.

At the pump: AAA's national average fell to $4.20 on Friday, about 8 cents below last weekend. AAA credits the switch to winter-blend fuel and says crude above $100 "is limiting further declines."

Starting Tuesday, Every Inbound Flyer Signs an Ebola Statement

The Chronicle Travel Desk · October 22–30, 2026

What happened: The CDC issued an order on October 22, amended it on October 28, and it takes effect at 11:59 p.m. Eastern on November 3 for flights departing foreign airports from that time. Everyone boarding an international flight to the United States must attest that they have not been in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in the previous 21 days, the longest time symptoms can take to appear. The order runs through December 18 unless changed. The CDC says it is "intended to reduce the risk of Ebola being introduced into the United States through air travel." No US cases have been reported, and the CDC calls the risk to travelers low.

Who is affected: Everyone on the plane: US citizens, green-card holders, visitors and crew, on commercial, private and general-aviation flights, including passengers only transiting the US. Travelers must attest even if they have never been near the DRC. Anyone who was there in the past 21 days, even on a connection, will be denied boarding without a government exemption. A parent or authorized adult signs for children under 18, and knowingly false statements can be prosecuted under federal law. Airlines for America, representing American, Delta, Southwest, United and JetBlue, asked the CDC on October 26 to delay the rule and exempt crews, calling it "too broad and too fast." IATA called the timeline "unreasonable." No delay had been announced as of Friday.

What to do: If you fly home from abroad on or after November 3, expect a paper or digital attestation at check-in. Watch for your airline's email or app prompt, allow extra time, and make sure an adult completes the form for each child. Anyone recently in central or East Africa should count carefully: the day you leave the DRC is day zero, and boarding is allowed on day 22.

For Atlantis clients: Clients returning from Europe, Mexico or the Caribbean are affected too, not only those coming from Africa. Add the attestation to every return-trip checklist and build in extra time at check-in for the first week of the rule.

Gulf Suspensions Now Run Into 2027

The Chronicle Travel Desk · October 23–30, 2026

What happened: Last week's Saudi story has widened. As of Friday, KLM has suspended Dubai until March 27, 2027, and Riyadh and Dammam until November 27. Cathay Pacific has suspended Dubai and Riyadh through January 31, 2027, and Air France Riyadh until December 12. Virgin Atlantic has suspended Dubai until October 2027, The National reported. British Airways plans to resume one daily flight each to Dubai and Riyadh on November 3. Saudi Arabia remains at Level 3, "Reconsider Travel," and non-emergency US staff and their families have been ordered out.

Who is affected: Anyone connecting through a Gulf hub. Closer to home, Delta's Atlanta–Tel Aviv route is suspended until December 18, and American is offering flexible rebooking for Doha travel through January 6, 2027.

What to do: Don't go to the airport without a confirmed, operating flight. Most carriers offer rebooking or refunds; ask for terms in writing. This week's warning that strikes on Iran could resume after November 3 (see International) is reason to avoid new Gulf bookings until the situation is clearer.

For Atlantis clients: Route around the Gulf for anything booked before the end of the year. Asia trips can connect through Europe or across the Pacific instead.

What the Filing Confirms, and What It Leaves Blank

Ozzy · Architecture Analysis · October 26–30, 2026

No. 17's teaser asked whether the S-1 would be filed or would slip again. It was filed. A day after that issue's Sunday cutoff, Anthropic announced the public filing on its newsroom and on X, five months after its confidential submission in June. As reported by Fortune and Yahoo Finance, the prospectus shows first-half 2026 revenue of $16.3 billion, against about $1.3 billion a year earlier, and full-year 2025 revenue of $4.6 billion. The first-half net loss was $80.7 billion under GAAP. About $72 billion of that is non-cash fair-value changes on convertible notes, SAFEs and derivatives. Fortune reports adjusted operating income of $1.6 billion for the half. Compute and infrastructure spending was about $9 billion in six months, against $7.3 billion in all of 2025, and two customers supply about a fifth of revenue.

One figure did not change status. The $16.8 billion third-quarter revenue figure No. 17 reported came from Anthropic's presentation to investors, and the S-1 runs only through June 30, so it remains unaudited. Compute commitments are reported differently by every outlet, from about $417 billion to as much as $637 billion including cancellable deals. This paper uses "more than $400 billion." The risk factors are unusually blunt. They warn of "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity," including model behavior aimed at self-preservation, and say Amazon, Google and Microsoft could curtail or reprice compute supply as they increasingly compete with Anthropic. Fortune confirms a dual-class structure. Third-party accounts of special founder and trust share classes have not been checked against the filing.

The filing leaves the price range, share count and raise blank. Talk of a $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion valuation and a raise of about $100 billion is press reporting, not company guidance; the May private round was at $965 billion post-money. ANSA, citing people familiar with the matter, reports a listing the week of November 16, "though timing could change." No amended S-1 with a range and no roadshow launch had been located as of Friday.

Big Tech's Bill Comes Due: Cloud Accelerates, Cash Turns Negative

Ozzy · AI Intelligence · October 26–30, 2026

The week's earnings showed the same pattern at every company: AI cloud demand is accelerating, and so is spending. Alphabet reported third-quarter revenue of $127.6 billion on Wednesday. Google Cloud revenue more than doubled to $30.7 billion, with a $561 billion backlog. But capex reached $51.4 billion for the quarter, free cash flow turned negative at $9.1 billion, and the company told investors to "expect a significant increase in capex in 2027." The shares fell 12.6% Thursday, erasing about $500 billion in market value. Amazon said Thursday that AWS grew 39% to about $46 billion, its fastest growth in nearly five years, with a $578 billion backlog, and that 2027 capex will be "meaningfully higher" than this year's roughly $220 billion; earnings missed and the stock fell about 7%. Microsoft reported Azure revenue in dollars for the first time, $33.2 billion, up 48% according to trade-press accounts of its results, and capex of $45.2 billion, below forecast because of third-party data-center delays.

Meta found another way to pay. On October 26 it launched about $35 billion of stock and mandatory convertible preferred offerings, according to its SEC filing, and Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom were named cornerstone investors with interest in up to $5 billion combined. Nvidia said it will put in $3 billion. So chip suppliers are now buying stock in a customer to fund that customer's chip purchases.
