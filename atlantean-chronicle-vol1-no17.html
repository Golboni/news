Oil Hits a Six-Month High as the Gulf War Widens Again

Iran's Revolutionary Guard says it struck "U.S.-linked" tankers, the Houthis hit Saudi Arabia's Dammam airport and an Aramco site at Yanbu, and President Trump says Washington may do "another little epic fury." Brent settled Friday at $116.72, its highest close since April 29. Talks run in parallel through Pakistan and Qatar, and both sides say they are not dead.
By The Chronicle International Desk · October 21–25, 2026

The week's escalation came from two directions at once. Late Wednesday, October 21, King Fahd International Airport in Dammam was attacked; Saudi Arabia's civil aviation authority reported one dead and eight injured, according to the AP, though other outlets put the toll at two. On Thursday the Houthis claimed that strike along with hits on a ministry building in Riyadh and an Aramco facility at the Red Sea port of Yanbu, and Aramco ordered the evacuation of its Dhahran headquarters, according to reports. The State Department ordered non-emergency US government staff and their families to leave Saudi Arabia, and Washington issued travel warnings covering 13 countries in the region.

The same day, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said it had struck four "U.S.-linked" tankers in the Gulf. Other tallies were higher, and none was independently confirmed. The UK Maritime Trade Operations center confirmed one: a tanker hit by an "unknown projectile" about seven nautical miles northeast of Limah, Oman, which continued its voyage. President Trump called Iran's claims fake, saying Tehran was inventing them to push up oil prices.

Markets did not wait to find out. Brent crude settled Friday at $116.72, up 3.1% on the day and more than 6% on the week, after briefly topping $117; West Texas Intermediate settled at $103.27. Two weeks ago Brent closed at $104.72. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Gulf crude exports are running at 17 to 18 million barrels a day, above pre-war levels, and that the US blockade of Iranian ports had "decimated" Iran's oil revenue; CENTCOM says the blockade has cost Iran at least $25 billion.

The rhetoric rose with the price. At a Fort Myers rally on Friday, Trump said, "We may do another little epic fury, or they'll agree," and called Hormuz "totally ours." Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said the Iranian government must be "overthrown once and for all." Reuters, citing two Iranian officials, reported that Tehran has plans to damage undersea cables if strikes resume.

The negotiating track runs alongside. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said indirect talks continue "without interruption" through Pakistan and Qatar, and that Washington had agreed to Iran's conditions but wants added clauses; under Iran's seven-day plan, Hormuz would fully reopen on day six. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said any deal must bar an Iranian nuclear weapon. Qatar's foreign ministry spokesman said on October 20 that "negotiation is ongoing," Pakistan's army chief visited Tehran, and IAEA chief Rafael Grossi told the UN General Assembly on Thursday that the agency is ready to monitor any agreement. None of it has produced a text.

After 14 years, Serbia's ruling party conceded to a student-led movement that grew out of the 2024 Novi Sad canopy collapse. Results are preliminary.

Serbia's Students Win: Vucic Concedes After 14 Years of His Party's Rule

The Chronicle International Desk · October 25, 2026

Serbia. Less than two hours after polls closed on Sunday, Aleksandar Vucic conceded Serbia's parliamentary election. "Based on the stratified sample, it is clear that we have lost the election," he said. Ipsos/CeSID projections gave the "Student List – Students Win" 52% of the vote, and Vucic said the list would take 135 to 140 of the 250 seats. Projections differ on his United Serbia coalition's share: one puts it at 39.3%, while a Euronews report also prints 32.3%. Turnout was reported as the highest for a parliamentary vote in more than 30 years. The result ends 14 years of rule by his Serbian Progressive Party. The movement grew out of the November 2024 canopy collapse at Novi Sad's railway station, which killed 16 people. Results are preliminary.

France and Italy. The cost-of-living protests that have run through France for two weekends spread further. On Saturday, October 24, the Interior Ministry counted 410,000 protesters nationwide, 110,000 of them in Paris; organizers claimed nearly 800,000. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said police were "at nearly 1,000 arrests." About 70,000 police and gendarmes were deployed. Lyon, Marseille, Nice and Toulon banned demonstrations, and the Louvre, the Musée d'Orsay and the Grand Palais closed. The previous Saturday drew about 550,000 marchers and more than 2,300 arrests. Nuñez offered to resign afterward, and President Macron refused. In Italy, Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni sent 500 more soldiers to Milan after looting near the Duomo, and her own defense minister objected.

Ukraine: Starlink Restored, a Local Truce, and Talks Set for November

The Chronicle International Desk · October 21–24, 2026

Starlink. Overnight October 23–24, Ukraine's Starlink and Starshield speeds were restored after a week of throttling that hampered front-line drone units. "The speeds are now where they need to be," President Zelensky said. On Saturday Elon Musk acknowledged that SpaceX had limited the service, saying Ukraine had used it for offensive drone strikes and that "that loophole was closed last week." President Trump said he did not make the call, and Secretary Rubio and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth denied any administration role.

Ceasefire track. The energy truce Trump announced on October 11 did not hold. Russia struck energy sites almost immediately, according to Kyiv; Ukraine held off for three days before resuming refinery strikes, and Moscow accused Kyiv of breaking the truce. In its place came smaller steps. Russia agreed on October 21 to resume three-way talks with the United States and Ukraine next month. On October 23, Kyiv and Moscow agreed to a localized ceasefire around Oleshky in the Kherson region, reportedly brokered by First Lady Melania Trump. Strikes continued elsewhere: on Friday Ukraine hit a chemical plant in Tolyatti, an electronics plant in Oryol, where local officials said three people were killed, and a target in Omsk, more than 2,000 kilometers from the border.

Nine Days Out: 24 Million Early Votes and a President at 31%

The Chronicle National Desk · October 19–25, 2026

Early voting for the November 3 midterms is running ahead of 2022. One national tracker counted 24.3 million ballots cast across 45 reporting states as of Saturday, about 13.2 million by mail and 11.1 million in person, up roughly 13% from the same point four years ago. ABC News, citing the University of Florida Election Lab, reported more than 18 million as of Thursday, and totals vary by tracker. In states that report party registration, registered Democrats account for about 44% of returns and Republicans about 31%. An earlier AP analysis found Texas up 74% from 2022 and Georgia past one million early votes, up 12%.

The ratings have moved one way. On October 9 the Cook Political Report shifted 18 House races toward Democrats and projected a Democratic gain of 9 to 19 seats. Republicans now hold the House 218–214, with two vacancies and one independent who caucuses with them, and the Senate 53–47, so Democrats need a net gain of four seats to win the Senate. President Trump's approval stood at 31% in a Reuters/Ipsos poll that closed October 19, the lowest of either of his terms, and 68% of respondents disapproved of his handling of the economy. Other polls put him between 32% and 36%. Coverage links the slide to the economy, gas prices and the Iran war.

The president is spending the final stretch in red states: Mississippi on Monday for Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith, who faces a tightening race against Democrat Scott Colom, then Texas, Ohio, Florida and Iowa.

Inflation Hit 3.7%. The Fed Is Expected to Wait Anyway — Until December

The Chronicle National Desk · October 14–25, 2026

September's consumer price index, released October 14, came in hotter than forecast: up 0.6% on the month and 3.7% on the year, against 3.4% in August and a consensus near 3.6%. Gasoline rose 9.1% in the month and accounted for more than half of the increase. Core prices rose 0.3% on the month and 2.6% on the year. White House economic adviser Kevin Hassett called the headline number "disappointing."

The Federal Open Market Committee meets Tuesday and Wednesday, with the decision due at 2 p.m. Eastern on October 28, followed by Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference. The target range has been 3.75%–4.00% since September's hike. Warsh said in mid-October that he does not "prejudge meetings," called October and December both decision points and said price pressures remain uncomfortably elevated. October hike odds fell after his remarks. CME FedWatch now puts a hold near 92%; a Reuters poll found 94 of 100 economists expect a hold, and all 100 expect a hike at the December 8–9 meeting.

Also next week: September PCE inflation and the advance estimate of third-quarter GDP arrive Thursday, October 29. The Atlanta Fed's GDPNow model tracks third-quarter growth at 3.5%.

Saudi Arabia: Airports Reopen, Attacks Continue, and Washington Orders Families Out

The Chronicle Travel Desk · October 18–25, 2026

What happened: Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport, closed after the October 10 strike, reopened to limited traffic between October 18 and 21. Airlines need prior approval from Saudi Arabia's civil aviation authority, and Saudia and flynas domestic flights were running by Wednesday. That night Dammam's King Fahd airport was attacked (see International). The State Department ordered non-emergency staff and their families to leave Saudi Arabia, kept the country at Level 3, “Reconsider Travel,” with the Yemen border area at Do Not Travel, and issued or updated advisories for 13 Middle East countries. Israel and Kuwait remain at Level 3 and Iraq at Level 4.

Who is affected: Anyone flying to or through Saudi Arabia, including Jeddah, Dammam, Abha, Taif and Madinah. Delta postponed its Atlanta–Riyadh nonstop, scheduled to launch October 23, to March 2027. Lufthansa has suspended Riyadh flights until November 18 and Dammam until November 30. British Airways plans to return November 3, and Air France–KLM has extended its suspension into November. Cathay Pacific has suspended service until January 31, 2027. Gulf carriers are running rolling cancellations.

What to do: Confirm every Saudi segment directly with the operating carrier, and confirm again inside 24 hours. Because the cause is security, cash compensation is unlikely, but refunds, rebooking and care such as meals and hotels usually still apply; ask for them in writing. Enroll in the State Department's STEP program.

For Atlantis clients: Anyone booked on the Delta nonstop needs a new plan now. Treat any reopening as provisional: a Saudi airport that is open on Monday can close again by Wednesday. Quote nothing in the kingdom through November without a written cancellation path.

The Priciest Holidays in a Decade — and a Fall of Daily Delays

The Chronicle Travel Desk · October 17–25, 2026

What happened: Hopper's 2026 Holiday Travel Index puts the average domestic Thanksgiving round trip near $402, up 31%, and Christmas near $452, up 23%. It calls this the most expensive holiday season in a decade and blames Mideast-driven jet fuel costs, route cuts and higher bag fees. Thanksgiving hotel rates are roughly flat at about $170 a night. Day-to-day flying is rough as well. Flight trackers logged about 3,287 delays and 121 cancellations nationwide on Thursday, October 23, with ground delays at Austin and San Francisco. The FAA remains about 3,000 air traffic controllers short, and the Transportation Department pushed its rule requiring airlines to disclose delay causes from October 19 to February 1, 2027.

Who is affected: Anyone flying home for the holidays, and anyone on a weekday afternoon connection this fall.

What to do: Book Thanksgiving now. Hopper's data favor flying on Thanksgiving Day, Black Friday or Cyber Monday, and returns on the Sunday after can top $600. Take the first flight of the morning where possible, and check fly.faa.gov before leaving for the airport.

For Atlantis clients: For drive-to trips, fares this high make the road the better value. Gas is $4.29 nationally, high but easing, and a Gatlinburg Thanksgiving for a family of four costs less by car than four $400 tickets.

What Investors Saw, What the Draft Says, and What Is Still Missing

Ozzy · Architecture Analysis · October 14–25, 2026

No. 16 asked whether a public S-1 would land by October 23. It did not. The investor meeting did happen. According to Reuters and the FT, Anthropic told prospective investors at its San Francisco headquarters that third-quarter revenue reached $16.8 billion, up from about $11.6 billion in the second quarter and $4.7 billion in the first, and that it was profitable on an adjusted basis. Quarter-on-quarter growth slowed to about 45% from roughly 147%. Reuters Breakingviews put the adjusted operating margin for the year through September at 11% and the gross margin at 85%.

A Wall Street Journal report on the draft S-1, published around October 21, added the other side of the ledger. First-half 2026 revenue was $16.3 billion, up from $1.1 billion a year earlier, against a net loss of $80.7 billion, about $72 billion of which was non-cash charges on financing instruments. First-half compute spending was about $9 billion, compute commitments reach up to $750 billion across Google, AWS and Microsoft, and two customers account for about 20% of revenue. Anthropic declined to comment. Reuters reported that some attendees discussed a valuation near $1.5 trillion, below earlier talk of about $2 trillion. The lead banks are reported to be Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Citigroup. Nasdaq is reported as the exchange but not confirmed, and no ticker has been announced.

The calendar is the story now. A confidential filer must make its S-1 public at least 15 days before the roadshow begins, so a roadshow the week of November 9 needs a public filing about now. Bloomberg has reported that the goal is to trade before Thanksgiving. Any filing early this week keeps that goal alive, and none at all moves the schedule again.
